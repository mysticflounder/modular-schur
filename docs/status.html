This page is the primary record of the modular Schur project: what is proven, what is machine-verified, what has been computed, what has been refuted, and what is open. It is updated as the research state changes rather than frozen at publication, so a claim’s status here supersedes any statement in the PDF.

Every claim below carries a status label. Proven means a complete proof exists and has been audited. Verified means the Lean kernel checks it and the axiom closure has been inspected. Certificate means a solver certificate established it in the private working tree; the certificate chain is not distributed and has not passed the public axiom audit, so it is not Proven. Empirical means a solver established it for specific parameters and nothing more. Conjecture means it is believed but unproven. Refuted means it was believed and is now disproven. Those are kept on the page deliberately.

The main theorem

For a modulus m, write S_m(k,\ell) for the largest N such that \{1,\ldots,N\} admits a k-partition into sets containing no solution to x_1 + \cdots + x_\ell \equiv y \pmod m, where x_1,\ldots,x_\ell and y all lie in the same class and repetitions among the x_i are allowed. This is the IsEllSumFree convention: \ell addends and a separate target.

Proven For all m \ge 2, \ell \ge 2, and k \ge m/\gcd(m,\ell-1) - 1,

S_m(k,\ell) = \frac{m}{\gcd(m,\ell-1)} - 1.

Verified The Lean statement is schurModResidue_eq in lean/ModularSchur/Partition.lean. The ModularSchur tree contains no sorry.

Formalization status

Twelve structural results are machine-checked in Lean 4, on toolchain leanprover/lean4:v4.28.0 with Mathlib pinned at 8f9d9cff.

They are checked through a comparator gate, which exists to stop a formalization from proving something weaker than it appears to. Each theorem is stated twice: once in comparator/Challenge.lean using only Mathlib definitions, with the proof left as sorry, and once in comparator/Solution.lean where it is proved from this project’s development. The comparator checks that the two elaborated declarations carry the same statement, so the Lean statement cannot quietly drift toward the project’s own convenient definitions. It then checks the permitted declaration and axiom boundary and replays the proof exports through the configured kernels. The source files are not byte-identical, and nothing compares them as bytes. The sorrys in Challenge.lean are the design: they are the twelve holes the gate fills.

Verified The gate last passed on public commit bae075f (run 32795126047, 2026-08-25 UTC). That commit is the one that pinned the workflow’s actions, the elan release, and the comparator checkout to fixed revisions, so the run also exercised the pinned workflow. No later public commit touches the gated paths (the lean/ package files, scripts/lake-build.sh, and the workflow file itself, as listed in the workflow’s path filter); the later commits, including the one that carries this page, change the site and metadata only. The build stamp in the page footer is the commit of the private source tree the public tree is synced from, not a public-repository SHA. The gate fails the build on any of: a sorryAx in the closure, an axiom outside {propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}, a Lean.ofReduceBool, or a Challenge/Solution statement mismatch. A second job replays the proofs through the independent nanoda kernel.

The comparator runs at its tag v4.28.0, matched to the Lean toolchain and pinned by commit in CI. The gate here is a self-audit of the project’s own trusted source, not a hostile-submission contest: CI hands the comparator a fully pre-built .lake, so the guarantee rests on the statement-identity, axiom-boundary, and kernel-replay checks rather than on the sandbox that exists to contain a malicious solution author. One open upstream issue questions that sandbox’s process containment (leanprover/comparator#77); it does not touch the checks this gate relies on.

One boundary note on sigmaInfty_le: the paper-side theorem that places every stable safe set of size at least 2 under that divisibility hypothesis (Theorem 8.1) is prose, not Lean; its formalization is deferred, and the Lean statement carries the hypothesis explicitly.

The main theorem as Lean sees it, with no project-specific definitions in the statement:

theorem schurMod_eq (m k ℓ : ℕ) (hm : 2 ≤ m) (hℓ : 2 ≤ ℓ)
    (hk : m / Nat.gcd m (ℓ - 1) - 1 ≤ k) :
    Nat.findGreatest
      (fun N => ∃ P : Fin k → Finset ℕ,
        (∀ x ∈ Finset.Ioc 0 N, ∃ i, x ∈ P i) ∧
        (∀ i j, i ≠ j → Disjoint (P i) (P j)) ∧
        (∀ i, P i ⊆ Finset.Ioc 0 N) ∧
        (∀ i, ∀ f : Fin ℓ → ℕ, (∀ a, f a ∈ P i) → ∀ y ∈ P i, (∑ a, f a) % m ≠ y % m))
      (m - 1) = m / Nat.gcd m (ℓ - 1) - 1

The audit boundary

An earlier computational route to some of these results lives in a generated tree of 7,478 Lean files (35 MB), of which 1,737 use native_decide. That route is excluded from the gate and is not part of the verification claim above: native_decide trusts the compiler rather than the kernel, so its closure carries Lean.ofReduceBool.

Verified The twelve theorems do not depend on that tree. Solution.lean imports only IntegerBridge, K1Theorem, and SigmaInfty; the resulting import cone reaches Basic, Partition, SingletonSafety, UniversalBound, UnifiedValue, UnifiedTheorem, and StableRange, and contains zero occurrences of native_decide. The structural proof subsumes the computational one; the generated tree is retained as history, not as evidence.

The public repository carries exactly that import cone: the ten modules in the figure below, the comparator files, and the build configuration. The paper’s Section 8 names SameSupportFiber.lean, CoordinateUnion.lean, WholeAxisPID.lean, and AnchoredExactTransversal.lean for its structural reductions. Those four modules exist only in the private working tree, and since the paper’s 2026-08-24 revision its introduction says so. They are not distributed, not inside the gate, and not part of the verification claim on this page, and nothing here speaks to their build state or axiom closure. See the errata.

Computational coverage

Direct SAT tables

scripts/schur_mod.py encodes S_m(k,\ell) as SAT and drives CaDiCaL. It has two modes. In certified mode (--certified) every UNSAT boundary must emit a DRAT proof that drat-trim replays, and the run fails closed when the proof or the checker is missing. The default exploratory mode may answer from an in-process solver with no certificate; its results are tabulations, not certificates.

Empirical Full grids computed for m = 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13 over k \le 12 and \ell \le 12, with probes out to \ell = 25. The encoder was validated first against the published values of D’orville et al. for m \in \{4,5,6,7\}, k \le 6, \ell \le 8 (168 cases, zero mismatches) and against brute force on 540+ small instances.

The full run produced 10,712 CNF/proof/solution triples spanning m \le 18, k \le 17, \ell \le 54. These artifacts are not distributed: they are local and gitignored. Only the boundary rows recorded in the phase tables carry an explicit proof_verified replay result. Treat the grids as reproducible rather than as shipped certificates.

The residual frontier program

Beyond the stabilized law, the project computes the threshold in the stable regime. For \ell \ge m-1 the safety of a class depends only on c = \ell \bmod m (the large-\ell coset criterion, the prose Theorem 8.1 named under sigmaInfty_le above), so k_0(m,\ell) is a function of (m,c) there; write k_0^\infty(m,c) for that value. It is this stable threshold, not the ordinary k_0(m,\ell), that is a function of the normalized cell; the paper’s Section 8 has stated the schema for k_0^\infty since its 2026-08-24 revision (the earlier form is recorded in the errata). The method caps each prime exponent of d, collapsing infinitely many moduli onto finitely many normalized cells, so an infinite family closes by a finite check.

Certificate-backed computation; public audit incomplete. The two results below rest on solver certificates and certificate theorems that live in the private working tree. They are not among the twelve gated theorems, the artifacts are not distributed, and the certificate sweep has not passed the axiom audit, so they carry the Certificate label, not Proven.

Certificate By finite normalized-cell reduction. 56 fixed-quotient lines (n = 36 through n = 345), each with per-cell certificates, governed by three capstone theorems. Stable laws for the families m = 36t (d=6), 72t (d=12), 72t (d=6t), 100t (d=10), 144t, 196t, 216t, 252t, 360t, computationally reproduced to t \le 500.

Certificate By explicit certificate. The rows those families do not cover are handled individually: 40 packing-gap rows for 8 \le m \le 60, then residual covers at successive frontiers.

The m \le 5500 scan reports zero residual rows. What that means, exactly: phase9_stable_tables.py --scan-residual-frontier 5500 computes \sigma^\infty and k_0^\infty for every row 8 \le m \le 5500, 1 < d < m, then drops three classes of rows before printing: rows with k_0^\infty \le \lceil (n-1)/\sigma^\infty \rceil, rows whose private-fragment certificate covers the cell by itself, and rows on a list of residual rows discharged in earlier phases that is hard-coded in the script (is_known_residual_row). Zero means that no row falls outside those three classes. The scan does not re-verify the earlier discharge of the listed rows, and no row-to-certificate manifest is published.

This is a statement about the stable regime \ell \ge m-1 and about k_0^\infty, not about the main theorem, which is unconditional and needs no computation.

Verification debt

Not everything computed is machine-checked to the standard of the twelve gated theorems. The certificate-theorem sweep over the m \le 5500 frontier has not been run through the axiom audit, and tauDPRec_characterization has no recorded axiom closure. Those results rest on their solver certificates, not on a kernel-checked Lean proof. No row-to-certificate manifest exists for the frontier: nothing public maps each of its rows to the theorem or certificate that discharges it.

Open questions

The threshold k_0(m,\ell)

The main theorem says what S_m(k,\ell) equals once k is large enough. The threshold, the least k at which the stable value is reached, is the open half of the problem.

Write n = m/\gcd(m,\ell-1) and let \sigma(m,\ell) be the largest size of a safe subset. Proven: k_0 \le n-1 when d < m, and, for n \ge 2, k_0 \ge \lceil (n-1)/\sigma \rceil. The lower bound needs n \ge 2: at \ell \equiv 1 \pmod m one has n = 1, the interval is empty and \sigma = 0.

For prime modulus the threshold is settled exactly, but only inside the hypotheses of the paper’s corollary: if p is prime, \ell \ge p-1, and \ell \not\equiv 1 \pmod p, then \sigma(p,\ell) = 1 and k_0(p,\ell) = p-1. That value is not new: under the same hypotheses, Corollary 8 of D’orville et al. gives S_p(k,\ell) = k for 1 \le k \le p-1 and S_p(k,\ell) = p-1 for k \ge p-1, so the stable value is first reached at k = p-1. What the paper adds is a second proof, by sumset saturation; since its 2026-08-24 revision the abstract says so and cites Corollary 8. Outside that range the threshold can be much smaller. At p = 5, \ell = 2 the partition \{1,4\} \sqcup \{2,3\} is safe, so k_0(5,2) = 2 rather than 4 (D’orville et al., Theorem 9: S_5(k,2) = 4 for k = 2, 3).

The natural conjecture is false at composite moduli. One might expect that for each m there is an L(m) beyond which k_0(m,\ell) = n-1 whenever d < m. Take m = 12 and any \ell \equiv 11 \pmod{12}, so d = 2 and n = 6. The pair C = \{1,5\} is \ell-safe: H_C = \langle 4\rangle has order 3, g_C = 4, and safety needs 4 \nmid 10. Directly, 11 \cdot \{1,5\} = \{3,7,11\}, disjoint from \{1,5\}. So \sigma \ge 2 and k_0(12,\ell) \le 3 < 5 = n-1. Such \ell are arbitrarily large, so no L(12) exists. The argument generalizes to any composite m with a suitable proper divisor; the correct replacement is coset-theoretic rather than singleton-based.

A second natural guess, k_0^\infty = \lceil (n-1)/\sigma^\infty \rceil for d > 1, is also refuted, first at m = 16, where \sigma^\infty = 2 gives a bound of 4 but the true value is 5.

Open The general d > 1 law that replaces it. The frontier program above establishes it row by row out to m \le 5500 without yielding a closed form.

Also open

  • A closed form for \sigma^\infty(m,c) as a function of (m,c), rather than a maximum over subsets.
  • The boundary regime 1 \le k < n-1. No unified formula; per-row eventual periodicity in \ell is conjectured, supported by the tables for 8 \le m \le 13.
  • \sigma(m,\ell) = \max\{\lvert H\rvert : H < \mathbb{Z}/m proper, \ell H \cap H = \varnothing\}. Conjecture.
  • A universal closed form for \tau, the residual minimum set cover. Open, and the anchored-transversal route to it is the one refuted above.

Erdős problem 483

This project does not address Erdős problem 483. The problem asks for the growth rate of the Schur numbers, whether f(k) < c^k, which no finite computation resolves. It is not the determination of S(6).

S(6) itself is unknown, with 536 \le S(6) \le 1836; the lower bound has stood since 2000. A recon pass on it concluded it was not a productive target for this project and it was abandoned. One lead was noted and not pursued: since h(r) \le f(r) and f(6) = S(6), any modular sum-free 6-colouring of [N] modulo N+1 certifies S(6) \ge N.

A planned Rado-number thread was never executed; the material that exists is a landscape memo, not a result.

D’orville et al. problems

This work engages two parts of Problem 1 of the originating paper. The numbering was checked against the published original on 2026-08-24 (Integers 25 (2025), #A62, Problem 1): part 3 asks for S_m(1,\ell) when 2 < \ell < (m+1)/2 and m \ge 8; part 5 asks for S_m(k,\ell) when m \ge 8. Part 3, the values of S_m(1,\ell) for 2 < \ell < (m+1)/2, m \ge 8, is closed by this work as S_m(1,\ell) = \min(\ell-1, \lfloor m/\ell \rfloor), formalized as schurModResidue_k1. Part 5’s large-k regime is settled by the main theorem. The parts not named here are not addressed by this work.

Refuted and retracted

These were working hypotheses in this project and are now known false. They stay on the page with their refuting evidence, because the cost of a dead end is paid twice if it is not recorded.

The anchored exact transversal route

Write \nu for the maximum packing and \theta for the axis cover of a fiber’s conflict structure. The anchored exact transversal hypothesis (AET) asserted \theta = \nu: that the cover is always tight.

Refuted On 2026-04-26, and independently re-certified on 2026-06-20, eight non-dyadic counterexamples closed the universal form. The cornerstone cases are n = 330 and n = 660, where \nu = 13 and \theta = 14.

The hypothesis was then narrowed to dyadic fibers, where it held across every case scanned. That restriction is also refuted (2026-06-21), by a single certified fiber:

n = 880,\quad d_0 = 35200,\quad P = (121,\,125,\,256) = (11^2,\,5^3,\,2^8)

with \nu = 48 and \theta = 50. The fiber is dyadic (880 = 55\cdot 2^4, no power of three anywhere), singleton-atom, PID-failing, and sub-saturating. The bounds are certified by cvc5 (maximum packing \ge 49 UNSAT, cover \le 49 UNSAT, cover of size 50 SAT), cross-checked against Z3 and CaDiCaL, with the encoding smoke-tested against the known n = 1716 and n = 330 results first.

What survives. The implication certificate shape \Rightarrow exactness is untouched and remains fully proved in Lean. What the counterexample removes is the claim that the shape disjunction is universal: that every PID-failing fiber has one of those shapes. Each individual shape still forces \theta = \nu. These are different statements and the second is not in question.

Retracted for solver error

Two reported dyadic AET failures, at n = 1760 and n = 3520, were false positives, caused by a maximum-independent-set routine that under-counted. AET in fact holds at both (\nu = \theta = 16). Six of fourteen reported non-dyadic failures came from the same defect and were withdrawn. The counterexamples that survive, including n = 880 above, were re-certified with the corrected procedure and by an independent audit path.

This is why the n = 880 result carries a cvc5 certificate and a second-solver cross-check rather than a scan report.

Errata in the paper

The paper and the PDF are the canonical text and are corrected only by their author. The statements below were wrong or under-scoped in the paper before its revision of 2026-08-24; each was checked against the source text and, where it is a computation, recomputed for this page. All of them are corrected in the current paper and PDF, whose Revision history section lists the changes; the entries stay here as the record of what was wrong and why.

Section 8, the K + \tau display. The paper wrote k_0(m,\ell) = K(d_0) + \tau(d_0) for the ordinary threshold and for any \ell with quotient n. As written that is false: (m,\ell) = (5,2) and (5,3) have the same d = 1, n = 5, d_0 = 1, but k_0(5,2) = 2 (\{1,4\} \sqcup \{2,3\} is safe) and k_0(5,3) = 4 (every two-element subset of \{1,2,3,4\} is unsafe for \ell = 3, so only singletons reach N = 4). Both values are in D’orville et al., Theorem 9. The quantity that depends only on the normalized cell is the stable threshold k_0^\infty(m,c) defined under the residual frontier program, and that is what the frontier scan computes (exact_k0_inf in phase9_stable_tables.py enumerates stable-regime safe sets). Section 8 now defines k_0^\infty and states the schema and the frontier for it; the remaining bare k_0 in Sections 8 and 9 refer to the ordinary threshold on purpose.

Abstract and Figure 8 caption: the prime threshold without its hypotheses. Both stated k_0(p,\ell) = p-1 with no hypotheses. The corollary in Section 5 has them, \ell \ge p-1 and \ell \not\equiv 1 \pmod p, and without them the claim is false: k_0(5,2) = 2. The abstract also presented the value as pinned down by this paper; under those hypotheses it follows at once from D’orville et al., Corollary 8, and the paper’s contribution is a second proof (see the threshold). Both statements now carry the hypotheses and the abstract cites Corollary 8.

Section 9, open problem 3 (optimizing over \ell). The paper said the pointwise value of S_m(k,\ell) at fixed k is supplied by the main theorem, so that maximizing over \ell is a divisor-counting problem. The main theorem applies only for k \ge n-1, and n depends on \ell. At m = 100, k = 1 the one-color theorem gives S_{100}(1,10) = \min(9, 10) = 9, while the main theorem at k = 1 covers only the \ell with n \le 2, where it gives at most 1. At fixed k the maximum over \ell is governed by the boundary regime 1 \le k < n-1, which is open (see Also open). The item now says so.

Section 5, the covering lower bound. Was stated without n \ge 2. At \ell \equiv 1 \pmod m one has n = 1, the set \{1,\ldots,n-1\} is empty, \sigma(m,\ell) = 0, and \lceil (n-1)/\sigma \rceil is undefined. The bound holds for n \ge 2, as stated on this page and now in the theorem.

Section 5, the gloss after the upper bound. The theorem says k_0(m,\ell) \le \max(1, n-1); the sentence after it said one never needs more than n-1 classes. At n = 1 the threshold is 1, not 0; the gloss now says so.

Figure 8 (saturation over a prime modulus). The caption said the sumsets of C = \{1,3\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/5 grow as 1C \subset 2C \subset \cdots. They do not: 2C = \{1,2,4\} does not contain 3 \in 1C. The chain is nested for D = C - a_0, which contains 0, and D is the set the proof uses. The caption now states the growth in size (which holds by Cauchy–Davenport) and names the shift; the panels, which show \ell C itself, were correct and are unchanged.

Section 6, the empirical count. The range 2 \le m \le 30, 2 \le \ell \le m has \sum_{m=2}^{30}(m-1) = 435 pairs; the paper said 414. All 435 agree with \min(\ell-1, \lfloor m/\ell \rfloor) (recomputed for this page), so the error was in the count only; the paper now says 435.

Section 8, coordinate terminology. Atoms were said to carry one coordinate per prime g in their support pattern. The labels are not always prime; the paper’s own axis tuples include 121, 125, and 256. The paper now says “axis label”.

Section 1, the formalization sentence. The paper said the main theorem, the k = 1 case, and the structural reductions of Section 8 are all formalized in Lean 4, and points to the public repository for the full development. The public repository carries the ten modules of the comparator import cone and nothing of Section 8; the four Section 8 modules the paper names are private and ungated (see the audit boundary). The sentence now names the public repository’s ten-module import cone and says the Section 8 modules are private and ungated.

Provenance

The quantity S_m(k,\ell) was introduced by Chappelon et al. (2013). The problems this work engages with are posed by D’orville, Sim, Wong, and Ho, Integers 25 (2025) #A62 (published version). The D’orville et al. attribution, theorem numbers, and problem numbering cited on this page were checked against that published version on 2026-08-24, and the Chappelon et al. attribution against Electron. J. Combin. 20(2) (2013), #P61 (arXiv:1306.5635) the same day; formalization.yaml and docs/references/ record both checks.

The Lean development was written by two AI systems under human direction. Claude (Anthropic), driven through Claude Code, wrote every definition, every theorem statement, the IntegerBridge and K1Theorem modules, and the comparator layer that restates the results with Mathlib definitions alone. Aristotle (Achim et al., 2025, arXiv:2510.01346) filled the proof bodies of 10 lemmas in the residue-side modules. Of the 12 theorems in the gate, Aristotle supplied the proofs behind seven; the integer-form theorems schurMod_eq, schurMod_is_greatest, no_valid_partition_of_ge_m, schurMod_eq_schurModResidue and the k = 1 theorem schurModResidue_k1 were proved in Claude-written modules. Every proof, whichever system produced it, is checked as described under Formalization status.
