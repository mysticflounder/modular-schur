title: "Prime-power structure of the stable regime for modular Schur numbers"
author: Adam McKenna
date: "September 2026 — draft"
abstract: |
  For a modulus $m$ and a sufficiently large sum length $\ell$, put
  $d=\gcd(m,\ell-1)$ and $n=m/d$. We prove that every nonempty safe subset
  of $[1,n-1]$ lies inside a canonical congruence block determined by one
  prime divisor of $n$, and that every such block is safe. The stable modular
  Schur problem therefore becomes an exact finite set-cover problem. We split
  this cover into mandatory support-one seeds and a residual cover, count the
  seeds in closed form, and show how multiplication by a prime transports the
  residual problem. Iteration removes every exponent beyond a canonical
  critical core and gives an explicit additive contribution for the removed
  prime-power layers. We also give exact prefix formulas for a prime-power
  core and a forced-block-plus-matching formula when exactly two active primes
  remain. The paper separates Lean-formalized results, audited prose proofs,
  and the open residual problem on critical cores with three or more axes.

# 1. Introduction

The modular Schur number $S_m(k,\ell)$, introduced in \cite{CMD2013}, is the largest $N$ for which
$1,\ldots,N$ can be colored with $k$ colors and no color contains

$$x_1+\cdots+x_\ell\equiv y\pmod m.$$

The endpoint formula established in the companion draft,
[*Endpoint formulas for modular Schur numbers, with a correction to a
prime-power formula*](paper-i-endpoint-formulas-draft.md), uses

$$d=\gcd(m,\ell-1),\qquad n=m/d$$

and shows that $S_m(k,\ell)=n-1$ once enough colors are available. The
present paper asks the next question: how many colors are actually necessary?

These questions belong to Ramsey theory on the integers and to additive
combinatorics; standard background appears in \cite{BB1982,LR2014}. The later
formulas, tables, and open problems of D'orville, Sim, Wong, and Ho
\cite{DSWH2025} provide the immediate literature context. The structural
prime-block results below are contributions of the present project.

For large $\ell$, this question has a rigid prime-power structure. Every safe
color class fits inside a congruence block attached to one prime divisor of
$n$. A coloring is therefore a cover of $[1,n-1]$ by these blocks. Some
blocks are mandatory because they contain a point found in no other block.
After deleting them, one smaller residual cover remains.

The main results are:

1. a canonical-block description of every safe set in the stable range;
2. an exact seed-plus-residual decomposition and a closed seed count;
3. an exact recurrence when a prime exponent is extended;
4. reduction to an exponent-truncated **critical core**;
5. exact formulas for prime-power prefixes and cores with at most one active
   prime; and
6. an exact bipartite-matching formula when two active primes remain.

No advanced algebra is assumed. We introduce valuations, cyclic subgroups,
set covers, and matchings as they appear. The main unresolved problem is also
stated precisely: after the forced prime-power tails are removed, determine
the residual cover on a critical core with three or more active prime axes.

# 2. Stable lengths and a coset lemma

A subset $C$ of the residues modulo $m$ is **$\ell$-sum-free** if it contains
no $x_1,\ldots,x_\ell,y$ with
$x_1+\cdots+x_\ell\equiv y\pmod m$. We work in the stable range

$$\ell\ge\max(2,m-1).$$

For a prime $p$ and positive integer $r$, the **$p$-adic valuation** $v_p(r)$
is the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $r$. This is only a
counting device here; no analysis over the $p$-adic numbers is used.

Choose $a\in C$ and let

$$H=\langle C-C\rangle$$

be the smallest subgroup of $\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$ containing all differences
$c-c'$. It has the form $g\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$ for a divisor $g$ of $m$.

**Lemma 2.1 (coset saturation).** If $|C|\ge2$ and $t\ge |H|-1$, then

$$tC=ta+H.$$

Consequently $C$ is unsafe at length $t$ precisely when
$g\mid(t-1)a$.

**Proof.** Put $D=C-a$. Then $0\in D$ and $D$ generates $H$. Because
$0\in D$, the chain $D\subseteq2D\subseteq3D\subseteq\cdots$ is increasing.
If a step fails to grow, say $jD+D=jD$, then translation by each member of
$D$ preserves $jD$. Hence translation by every member of the subgroup
generated by $D$ preserves it, forcing $jD=H$. Before that happens the size
increases at every step, so it happens by step $|H|-1$. Translating back gives
$tC=ta+H$.

All of $C$ lies in $a+H$. The cosets $a+H$ and $ta+H$ meet precisely when
$(t-1)a\in H$, which says $g\mid(t-1)a$. $\square$

**Theorem 2.2 (stable dependence on the gcd).** Let
$\ell,\ell'\ge\max(2,m-1)$ and suppose

$$\gcd(m,\ell-1)=\gcd(m,\ell'-1)=d.$$

Every subset of residues has the same safety status at lengths $\ell$ and
$\ell'$. Therefore $S_m(k,\ell)=S_m(k,\ell')$ for every $k\ge1$.

**Proof.** Write $m=dn$, $\ell-1=du$, and $\ell'-1=du'$. Then both $u$ and
$u'$ are coprime to $n$. A singleton $\{x\}$ is unsafe at either length
precisely when $n\mid x$.

For $|C|\ge2$, use Lemma 2.1 and write $H=g\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$. Put
$e=\gcd(g,d)$ and $h=g/e$. From $g\mid dn$ one obtains $h\mid n$. Also
$g\mid dux$ holds precisely when $h\mid x$, because $h$ is coprime to both
$d/e$ and $u$. The same calculation with $u'$ gives the same test. Thus each
class, each partition, and each feasible prefix has the same status at both
lengths. $\square$

We may therefore write $S^\infty_{m,d}(k)$ for the common stable value.

# 3. Canonical prime blocks

Assume $n=m/d\ge2$ and put

$$U_n=[1,n-1].$$

For every prime $p$, define

$$a_p=v_p(d),\qquad b_p=v_p(n).$$

Thus $b_p=0$ when $p\nmid n$. Canonical blocks themselves use only primes
dividing $n$.

If $x\in U_n$ and $t=v_p(x)<b_p$, define the canonical block

$$
B_{p,x}=\{y\in U_n:y\equiv x\pmod {p^{a_p+t+1}}\}.
$$

Different anchors can name the same subset. When counting blocks, duplicate
subsets are counted once unless an axis label is explicitly retained.

**Theorem 3.1 (canonical-block normal form).**

1. Every canonical block is $\ell$-sum-free.
2. Every nonempty $\ell$-sum-free set $C\subseteq U_n$ is contained in a
   canonical block.

**Proof.** Fix $B_{p,x}$ and put $t=v_p(x)$. Every difference of two members
of the block is divisible by $p^{a_p+t+1}$. If the subgroup generated by the
differences is $g\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$, then
$v_p(g)\ge a_p+t+1$. After removing the common factor with $d$, the reduced
divisor $h=g/\gcd(g,d)$ has $v_p(h)\ge t+1$, so $h\nmid x$. The divisibility
test in the proof of Theorem 2.2 and Lemma 2.1 show that the block is safe.
The singleton case follows directly from $x<n$.

Conversely, take a nonempty safe $C$ and $x\in C$. For a singleton, choose
a prime $p\mid n$ for which $v_p(x)<b_p$; one exists because $n\nmid x$.
Then $C\subseteq B_{p,x}$.

If $|C|\ge2$, write $\langle C-C\rangle=g\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z$ and
$h=g/\gcd(g,d)$. Safety and Lemma 2.1 imply $h\nmid x$, while the calculation
in Theorem 2.2 gives $h\mid n$. Choose $p$ with $v_p(h)>v_p(x)=t$. Then
$v_p(g)\ge a_p+t+1$. Every difference $y-x$, with $y\in C$, is divisible by
$g$ and therefore by $p^{a_p+t+1}$. Hence every $y$ lies in $B_{p,x}$.
$\square$

For $0\le N\le n-1$, let $\kappa_{m,d}(N)$ be the smallest number of
distinct canonical blocks whose union contains $[1,N]$.

**Corollary 3.2 (exact prefix reduction).** The smallest number of safe
classes needed to partition $[1,N]$ is $\kappa_{m,d}(N)$. Consequently

$$
S^\infty_{m,d}(k)=
\max\{0\le N\le n-1:\kappa_{m,d}(N)\le k\}.
$$

**Proof.** A safe partition can be enlarged class by class to a cover by
canonical blocks using Theorem 3.1, so the cover number is no larger. In the
other direction, assign each point to one selected block containing it,
breaking ties arbitrarily. The assigned subsets partition the prefix and are
safe because they lie inside safe blocks. $\square$

Write $\kappa(n,\mathbf a)=\kappa_{m,d}(n-1)$ when only the quotient $n$ and
the depth data $\mathbf a=(a_p)$ matter.

# 4. Mandatory seeds and the residual cover

We first give a general set-cover lemma. Let $L$ be a finite set of labels and
let $N(i)$ be the set of points covered by label $i$. Two labels may have the
same neighborhood. A label is a **seed** if it has a private point: some
$x\in N(i)$ belongs to no $N(j)$ with $j\ne i$.

Let $F$ be the set of seed labels. Delete every point covered by a seed and
restrict every nonseed neighborhood to the remaining points. Let
$\tau_{\rm res}$ be the minimum size of a cover of this residual universe.

**Lemma 4.1 (seed deletion).** The original cover number is

$$\tau=|F|+\tau_{\rm res}.$$

**Proof.** Every cover must select each member of $F$, because its private
point has no other covering label. After these mandatory labels are chosen,
exactly the residual universe remains. Adding a minimum residual cover gives
a cover of size $|F|+\tau_{\rm res}$, while restricting any original cover
after its forced labels are removed gives the opposite inequality. Private
points also show that two seeds cannot have identical neighborhoods, so no
duplicate-set count is hidden in $|F|$. $\square$

For canonical blocks, a point $x$ lies on the $p$-axis when
$v_p(x)<b_p$. Its **support** is the set of all such prime axes. The seeds are
exactly the blocks having a point whose support consists of one prime.

**Theorem 4.2 (closed seed count).** Define

$$
K_{\rm seed}(n,\mathbf a)=
\sum_{p\mid n}(p-1)\sum_{j=0}^{b_p-1}p^{\min(a_p,j)}.
$$

Then the number of canonical seed blocks is $K_{\rm seed}(n,\mathbf a)$, and

$$
\boxed{\kappa(n,\mathbf a)=K_{\rm seed}(n,\mathbf a)+\tau_{\rm res}(n,\mathbf a).}
$$

**Proof.** A support-one witness on the $p$-axis is divisible by
$q^{b_q}$ for every other prime $q\mid n$. Put $M=n/p^{b_p}$. Every such
witness at layer $t$ has the unique form

$$x=Mp^t u,\qquad 1\le u<p^{b_p-t},\qquad p\nmid u.$$

After division by $p^t$, its block is determined by $Mu$ modulo
$p^{a_p+1}$. Since $M$ is coprime to $p$, multiplication by $M$ permutes the
unit residues. The range contains $(p-1)p^{b_p-t-1}$ possible units, while a
complete unit residue system modulo $p^{a_p+1}$ has $(p-1)p^{a_p}$ members.
There is no repetition before the smaller of these two counts is reached.
Thus the number of represented blocks is

$$
(p-1)p^{\min(a_p,b_p-t-1)}
$$

such residues. Blocks at different layers or on different axes have different
private witnesses. Summing over $t=0,\ldots,b_p-1$ and reversing the index by
$j=b_p-t-1$ gives the displayed inner sum. Lemma 4.1 then gives the cover
decomposition. $\square$

# 5. Multiplying by a prime

Let $p$ be prime, let $p^{b_p}$ be the exact power of $p$ dividing $n$, and
assume

$$a_p\le b_p.$$

This is the **stable-depth** condition. Let $C_n$ be the points covered by
seeds and $R_n=U_n\setminus C_n$ the residual points.

**Theorem 5.1 (residual transport).** Under the stable-depth condition,

$$
C_{pn}=\{y\in U_{pn}:p\nmid y\}\ \sqcup\ \{px:x\in C_n\},
$$

and hence

$$\boxed{R_{pn}=\{px:x\in R_n\}.}$$

Moreover

$$
\tau_{\rm res}(pn,\mathbf a)=\tau_{\rm res}(n,\mathbf a).
$$

**Proof.** First suppose $p\mid n$. Multiplication by $p$ preserves the full
list of supported prime axes: on the $p$-axis both the point valuation and the
ambient exponent rise by one; on every other axis the valuation is unchanged.
Thus $px$ has a support-one witness precisely when $x$ does.

If $p\nmid n$, then $b_p=0$, so the depth assumption forces $a_p=0$. The old
universe has no $p$-axis. After multiplication by $p$, each transported old
point has $p$-valuation at least the new ambient exponent and remains
unsupported on that axis. Its old support is again unchanged.

Every new point not divisible by $p$ lies at $p$-valuation zero. The inequality
$a_p\le b_p$ ensures that every represented valuation-zero $p$-block has a
support-one witness, so all such points are seed-covered. This proves the two
point-set identities.

Write a canonical label as $(q,t,r)$: its prime axis, valuation layer, and
residue. Define

$$
\Psi(q,t,r)=
\begin{cases}
(p,t+1,pr),&q=p,\\
(q,t,pr\bmod q^{a_q+t+1}),&q\ne p.
\end{cases}
$$

The first branch is absent when $p$ is fresh. The residual neighborhood of
$\Psi(q,t,r)$ is exactly the pointwise multiple by $p$ of the old residual
neighborhood. Conversely, every **nonempty** new residual neighborhood has an
old producer: choose a point $px$ in it and take the old label through $x$ on
the same axis.

Send an old minimum cover forward and discard duplicate extensional
neighborhoods; its size cannot increase. In the reverse direction, discard
selected labels with empty neighborhoods, choose one old producer for each
remaining neighborhood, and again discard duplicates. Its size cannot
increase either. The two minimum cover sizes are equal. $\square$

The seed count increases by one full stabilized layer:

$$K_{\rm seed}(pn,\mathbf a)=K_{\rm seed}(n,\mathbf a)+(p-1)p^{a_p}.$$

Combining this with Theorems 4.2 and 5.1 gives the main recurrence.

**Corollary 5.2 (one-step recurrence).**

$$
\boxed{\kappa(pn,\mathbf a)=\kappa(n,\mathbf a)+(p-1)p^{a_p}.}
$$

The condition $a_p\le b_p$ is necessary. Below that depth, new nonmultiples
of $p$ need not be seed-covered, so the residual point identity can fail.

# 6. Inactive primes and prime-power cores

Call $p\mid n$ **active** when $a_p>0$ and **inactive** when $a_p=0$. Factor

$$n=n_A n_I,$$

where $n_A$ contains the active prime powers and $n_I$ the inactive ones. Put

$$d_A=\prod_{p\mid n_A}p^{a_p},\qquad m_A=d_An_A,$$

and use the convention that the terminal cover of $n_A=1$ is $0$.
If $p^b\parallel n_I$, its one-axis blocks are indexed by a layer
$0\le t<b$ and a nonzero residue modulo $p$, giving $b(p-1)$ mandatory
blocks.

**Theorem 6.1 (inactive-prime stripping).** At the terminal prefix,

$$
\boxed{\kappa(n,\mathbf a)=
\sum_{p^b\parallel n_I}b(p-1)+\kappa_{m_A,d_A}(n_A-1).}
$$

When $n_A=1$, the final term is $0$.

**Proof.** For each inactive block, choose a point divisible by every other
prime power and lying in the prescribed $p$-layer and nonzero residue. This
point lies on only that axis, so the block is mandatory. Their union is exactly
the set of points not divisible by $n_I$. The remaining points are

$$n_I\,[1,n_A-1].$$

Division by $n_I$ preserves incidence with every active canonical block.
Thus the residual cover is $\kappa_{m_A,d_A}(n_A-1)$, while the mandatory inactive
blocks contribute the displayed sum. $\square$

For a prime-power quotient $n=p^b$, define

$$
R_t(N)=\left\lfloor\frac N{p^t}\right\rfloor-
       \left\lfloor\frac N{p^{t+1}}\right\rfloor,
$$

the number of integers at most $N$ with $p$-valuation exactly $t$, and

$$
F_{p,a,b}(N)=\sum_{t=0}^{b-1}
\min\bigl((p-1)p^a,R_t(N)\bigr).
$$

**Theorem 6.2 (prime-power prefix formula).** If $n=p^b$ and $a=v_p(d)$,
then for $0\le N\le p^b-1$,

$$\boxed{\kappa_{m,d}(N)=F_{p,a,b}(N).}$$

Therefore

$$
S^\infty_{m,d}(k)=
\max\{0\le N\le p^b-1:F_{p,a,b}(N)\le k\}.
$$

**Proof.** There is only one prime axis, so every point belongs to exactly one
block on that axis and every represented block must be selected. At layer $t$,
there are $R_t(N)$ points. After division by $p^t$, the relevant quotients are
the first $R_t(N)$ positive integers not divisible by $p$. Their residues run
through the nonmultiples of $p$ modulo $p^{a+1}$, a cycle of $(p-1)p^a$
labels with no repetition before the cycle closes. The number represented is
therefore the
minimum in the definition of $F$. Summing the disjoint layers proves the
formula. $\square$

Define the stable terminal threshold by

$$k_0^\infty(m,d)=\kappa_{m,d}(n-1).$$

At $N=p^b-1$ this becomes

$$
k_0^\infty(m,d)=
(p-1)\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}p^{\min(a,j)}.
$$

For $p=2$ and $a=0$, the prefix formula simplifies to

$$S^\infty_{m,d}(k)=\min(2^k-1,2^b-1).$$

# 7. The exponent-truncated critical core

The recurrence from Section 5 removes every exponent layer lying beyond its
depth in $d$. Define

$$
n_{\rm crit}=\prod_{p\mid n}p^{\min(b_p,a_p)},
$$

and let

$$e_p=b_p-\min(b_p,a_p)$$

be the number of removed layers on the $p$-axis.

**Theorem 7.1 (critical-core reduction).**

$$
\boxed{
\kappa(n,\mathbf a)=\kappa(n_{\rm crit},\mathbf a)+
\sum_{p\mid n}e_p(p-1)p^{a_p}.}
$$

**Proof.** Fix a prime $p$. Once the current exponent of $p$ is at least
$a_p$, Corollary 5.2 says that adding one further factor of $p$ adds exactly
$(p-1)p^{a_p}$ to the cover number. Starting at exponent
$\min(b_p,a_p)$ and repeating this step $e_p$ times removes the entire excess
$p$-tail. When $a_p=0$, the truncated core initially omits $p$; its first
insertion is the fresh-prime case of Theorem 5.1 and contributes $p-1$, as the
same formula requires. Do this for every prime divisor of $n$. Prime multiplication
commutes, and the added terms depend only on the chosen prime and its fixed
depth, so the contributions add to the displayed sum. $\square$

The critical core is not a guessed smaller modulus. It is the unique quotient
obtained by truncating each exponent $b_p$ at the depth $a_p$ supplied by
$d$. All removed layers are completely understood. The remaining term
$\kappa(n_{\rm crit},\mathbf a)$ contains the genuine interaction among prime
axes.

# 8. Two active primes and bipartite matching

Assume the active pair is

$$n=p^bq^c,\qquad d_A=p^aq^e,\qquad m_A=d_An,$$

for distinct primes $p,q$, with $a=v_p(d)>0$ and $e=v_q(d)>0$. Fix a prefix
$[1,N]$. A point supported on only one axis forces its unique block. Let
$\mathcal F_N$ be the distinct forced blocks and delete all points they cover.

Every remaining point lies in one surviving $p$-block and one surviving
$q$-block. Make a bipartite multigraph $G_N$: the left vertices are surviving
$p$-blocks, the right vertices are surviving $q$-blocks, and each residual
point is an edge joining its two blocks. Parallel edges are allowed.

Use one labelled vertex for each distinct block on each axis. If a $p$-block
and a $q$-block happen to be the same extensional subset, retain both axis
labels while constructing the graph.

Let $\nu(G_N)$ be the largest number of edges with no shared endpoint.

**Theorem 8.1 (two-axis matching formula).**

$$\boxed{\kappa_{m_A,d_A}(N)=|\mathcal F_N|+\nu(G_N).}$$

**Proof.** Every forced block must be selected. The labelled-cover lemma first
chooses representatives when labels have duplicate extensional neighborhoods;
forced blocks on opposite axes cannot coincide because their private witnesses
have different support. After their points are deleted, selecting represented
canonical blocks is selecting graph vertices that touch every edge: a vertex
cover. Opposite-axis labels for the same remaining subset are identified when
the extensional cover cardinality is counted. Thus

$$\kappa_{m_A,d_A}(N)=|\mathcal F_N|+\tau(G_N),$$

where $\tau$ is the minimum vertex-cover size.

For completeness, we recall the finite bipartite matching argument. Choose a
maximum matching $M$. Starting from unmatched left vertices, follow alternating
paths: unmatched edges from left to right and matched edges from right to left.
Let $Z$ be the reached vertices. Then

$$C=(L\setminus Z)\cup(R\cap Z)$$

covers every edge. Otherwise an uncovered edge would extend an alternating
path, or contradict the way a matched left vertex was reached. No unmatched
right vertex is reached, since that would give an augmenting path and a larger
matching. Each edge of $M$ has exactly one endpoint in $C$, so
$|C|=|M|$. Every vertex cover has at least $|M|$ vertices because the edges of
$M$ are disjoint. Hence $\tau(G_N)=\nu(G_N)$, proving the formula. Parallel
edges do not change either quantity. $\square$

The forced contribution is explicit. With

$$
F_{r,u,h}(M)=\sum_{t=0}^{h-1}
\min\left((r-1)r^u,
\left\lfloor\frac M{r^t}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac M{r^{t+1}}\right\rfloor\right),
$$

one has

$$
|\mathcal F_N|=
F_{p,a,b}\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac N{q^c}\right\rfloor\right)+
F_{q,e,c}\!\left(\left\lfloor\frac N{p^b}\right\rfloor\right).
$$

Indeed, a point is supported only on the $p$-axis precisely when it is
divisible by $q^c$; division by $q^c$ reduces its block count to the
prime-power calculation of Theorem 6.2. The other term is symmetric.

At the terminal prefix,

$$
k_0^\infty(m,d)=
(p-1)\sum_{j=0}^{b-1}p^{\min(a,j)}+
(q-1)\sum_{j=0}^{c-1}q^{\min(e,j)}+
\nu(G_{n-1}).
$$

# 9. What remains open

Theorems 4.2 and 7.1 separate the terminal cover into three parts:

$$
\text{forced seed count}
+\text{explicit exponent-tail contribution}
+\text{critical residual cover}.
$$

The first two parts have closed formulas. One active prime is handled by
Section 6, and two active primes are handled by bipartite matching in Section
8. With three or more active axes, a residual point may belong to three or
more blocks. The graph becomes a hypergraph, and the bipartite matching proof
no longer applies.

The central open problem is:

> Determine the residual cover number on every exponent-truncated critical
> core with at least three active prime axes.

No universal equality between cover and packing numbers is asserted here.
Such an equality is false in general, including in certified project examples.
Finite computations and special-cell certificates are useful evidence but do
not replace a universal theorem.

# 11. Conclusion

The stable modular Schur problem is not an unstructured search over colorings.
It is an arithmetic cover whose blocks are indexed by prime, valuation layer,
and residue. Private points force a computable seed contribution. Stable prime
multiplication preserves the residual cover and adds a fixed amount. Iterating
that fact strips the quotient down to a canonical critical core.

This gives a sharp division of labor. Prime-power tails are solved by a closed
formula. A one-axis core is explicit, and a two-axis core is a matching
problem. The remaining difficulty is concentrated on the finite but genuinely
multi-axis residual cover of the critical core.
